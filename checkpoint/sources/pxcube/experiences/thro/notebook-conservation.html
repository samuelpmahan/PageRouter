The thro on the left is the real site running on a throwaway workspace in memory: your saved work and settings are never read or written. Use the card yourself or run the gauge; every gesture is booked.

The laws

  1. Gestures are conserved. Every gesture that begins ends exactly once and says how: painted its pixels, superseded by a newer gesture, unchanged (nothing to redraw), or failed. So begun = painted + superseded + unchanged + failed + open, and open is 0 once things settle. A gesture still open after 3 seconds is a violation, counted.
  2. Time balances. Every gesture is a PxC run and every stage a kernel call, so the kernel times them; nothing here keeps its own clock. A gesture's total, from the finger until its last work (the save) is done, is its stages plus its remainders. Each remainder is named by where it sits (between which two stages), not by a guess at what is inside it, and kept signed. Stages that ran at the same time are not sequential: the overlap is shown, not hidden. The white mark is when its pixels were on screen. A stage that spends 5% or more of the gesture in its own code, not itemised, is flagged opaque.
  3. Final pixels are painted exactly once. A gesture that paints puts its final pixels on screen once; painting the same pixels again in a row is a duplicate.
